    Course Content.

    This course explores various mathematical topics taught in middle and high school from an advanced perspective with an emphasis on geometry. We will see how middle/high school concepts evolve at higher levels, and explore connections between different areas of mathematics by identifying underlying structure and principles. Anticipated topics include rational, real, and complex numbers; transformational geometry and congruence; distance and similarity; trigonometry; and area. This is a proof-based course! You are expected to be comfortable understanding and writing proofs using a variety of techniques.
    🔗
    🔗

    Course Technology Requirements.

    We will be using an online homework platform to submit written work electronically, so you will need to be fluent with basic technological tasks such as accessing the internet, uploading documents, and scanning written work into a PDF.

You will need to create a MOM account and register for our course. We will not follow any particular textbook, rather a collection of instructor-created notes on the covered topics will be provided.

    Subsection Assignments and Grading

    Standards Based Grading.

    In this course, we will be using standards based grading, which is an alternative approach to grading that emphasizes progress and growth rather than an arbitrary weighted average of all assessments. Instead of using a percentage-based system, your grade will be determined by how well you display mastery of specific sorts of mathematical tasks called Learning Targets (LTs). This type of grading system allows students to make up for past poor performances by allowing for a limited number of reassessments without penalty, provides real-time feedback on their current understanding of course objectives, and directs and emphasizes long-term progress in key course objectives. All aspects of the course and its grading scheme will focus around the 22 LTs presented at the end of this syllabus. In short, your grade will be determined by your performance in four categories: class prep assignments, written homework sets, in-class quizzes, and a cumulative final exam; detailed information about each of these categories, as well as how your course grade will be determined using these categories, can be found in the following sections of this syllabus. You will likely find this system to be new and unfamiliar, so I encourage you to ask questions about this approach to grading as soon as they arise! Please see the ``Advantages of Standards Based Grading’’ section of this syllabus for more information about why we are using this system in this course!
    🔗
    🔗

    Class Prep Assignments.

    There will be a class prep assignment due before most class meetings consisting of a short reading introducing the topic for that class, followed by some basic questions asking you to demonstrate your understanding of the reading. The class prep assignments will be due by 11:59pm on Mondays and Wednesdays (i.e., the night before each class meeting). No late attempts will be accepted for any reason. Class prep assignments will be graded on completeness and effort (as determined by the instructor) only and will be weighted equally.
    🔗
    🔗

    Homework.

    Homework will be assigned to both provide practice of concepts covered in class as well as extend those concepts beyond what is done in class. Three (or sometimes more) homework problems will be assigned for each course LT after the topic is discussed in class. Each homework set will be posted on MOM and need to be submitted as a single PDF there. Your homework solutions can be handwritten (in which case you will need to scan your pages into a single PDF using a scanner or scanner app), written using a tablet’s note app (in which case you will need to export your work as a PDF), or typeset with LaTeX (in which case a single PDF is automatically generated). All homework assignments will by due by 11:59pm on the Wednesday after they are assigned. To account for any unexpected emergencies or technical issues that may occur, we will utilize the LatePass feature in MOM. A LatePass can be redeemed for an automatic 48-hour extension on a homework assignment; to use a LatePass, click the ``Use LatePass’’ button next to the appropriate assignment on MOM, and the platform will automatically extend the due date for that assignment to 48 hours after the original due date. Each student will be given 11 LatePasses to use as they wish throughout the semester. A maximum of two LatePasses can be used on any single assignment. If circumstances require a more substantial extension, contact your instructor to discuss this possibility.
    🔗
    You are welcome (and encouraged!) to discuss the homework with each other, though everyone must submit their own solutions. I encourage you to take advantage of my office hours! Under no circumstances should you look for answers online -- submitting answers obtained online constitutes academic dishonesty and will be handled accordingly (see the ``Academic Honesty’’ section below for more details!). All submitted work must reflect your personal understanding of each solution!
    🔗
    Each problem will be graded individually and will receive a mark of either ``S’’ (Success), ``R’’ (Revisable), or ``N’’ (New attempt needed), according to the following rubric:
    🔗
    Score Description
    S Solution meets all of the following specifications:
    • Mathematically correct, well-organized, logic is easy to follow.
      🔗
    • Written in paragraph form (except calculations) with proper spelling/grammar.
      🔗
    • All definitions and theorems properly cited.
      🔗
    • Uses proper mathematical notation and terminology.
      🔗
    • Written at a level appropriate for your classmates to follow from beginning to end.
      🔗
    • Any error is trivial and does not affect the solution.
      🔗
    R Solution meets all of the specifications for an "S" except for a minor issue needing clarification or correction. Solution can be revised with minor edits or additions.
    N Solution does not meet one or more of the specifications for an "S".
    A homework LT is considered "complete" when you have earned an "S" on three problems for that LT; for most LTs, this will be all assigned problems. If you don’t earn an "S" on your initial submission, that’s ok! The mark ("R" or "N") tells you what you need to do to improve your mark:
    🔗
    • If you receive an "R", come to an office hour or make an appointment with me to discuss your solution. Explain what happened and show me how to fix it. If you convince me, the "R" will turn into an "S". No formal resubmission is necessary!
      🔗
    • If you receive an "N", rework the solution completely accounting for all provided feedback and resubmit it for grading through MOM. Your mark on the resubmission will replace the original mark.
      🔗
    You may reassess up to two homework problems per week; this can include either two "R"s, two "N"s, or one of each.
    🔗
    🔗

    LT Quizzes.

    We will have a quiz at the end of most Thursday classes. Each quiz question will address a single LT; you will receive a score of 0-4 for each individual question based on how well you showed your understanding of the corresponding LT according to the following rubric:
    🔗
    Score Description
    4 Fundamental and thorough understanding of the LT exceeding expectations. Provides clear understanding with complete and clear communication. Any error is trivial.
    3 Adequate understanding of the LT meeting expectations. Provides an adequate understanding with correct work and clear explanations. Some revisions or expansion is needed, but no significant gaps or errors are present.
    2 Partial understanding of LT falling below expectations. Provides a partial understanding, but significant gaps remain. Needs further work, more review, and/or improved explanation.
    1 Little understanding of LT falling significantly below expectations. Provides little to no understanding with either fragmentary work, significant errors or omissions, or too many errors.
    0 No understanding of the LT or LT was not attempted.
    In order to continually emphasize progress and growth, you may try to improve your score on any LT by reassessing on it. Quiz reassessments can take place during my office hours, at other times I set aside for reassessments, or during future quizzes. Any reassessment must be scheduled at least 24 hours in advance using the appropriate online form to ensure I will have the appropriate reassessment ready! Note, you must submit this form 24 hours prior in order to be allowed to reassess; simply showing up at my office will not suffice. You may do up to two reassessments per week. Our last class meeting on April 30 will be a dedicated ``Reassessment Day’’. The class time will be dedicated to allowing students to reassess up to 10 LTs each in addition to the usual LT quiz for that class meeting.
    🔗
    🔗

    Final Exam.

    The third category is a cumulative final exam, which is scheduled for Thursday, May 7, 12:30 -- 3:30pm, the time indicated on the WSU Spring 2026 Academic Calendar. The final exam will consist of 14 problems, each associated with one of the 22 LTs for this course. Each problem will be scored using the same rubric (0--4) as the homework/quiz problems. There will be no reassessments for the final exam! A make-up final exam will be given at the instructor’s discretion, and only when there is a legitimate and documented reason for missing the original final exam. Working or arriving late for class are not legitimate reasons for missing an exam!

    Advantages of Standards Based Grading.

    This grading system may be unfamiliar to you, and therefore cause some level of confusion or stress. Please try to keep an open mind about this grading system and remember things are not bad simply because they are unfamiliar! From my point of view as a mathematics professor who has taught a vast number of courses for over a decade, including this one multiple times, there are three major advantages of this standards based grading system over a traditional weighted-average system:
    🔗
    1. You will have several opportunities to display your understanding of the material. You will be assessed on each of the LTs twice on quizzes and have the ability to reassess on them, so you do not need to worry if you do not completely understand a LT the first time you encounter it! If it takes you a bit longer to grasp a concept, you will not be penalized as long as you make the effort to reassess!
      🔗
      🔗
    2. This approach stresses working out problems in a completely correct way to emphasize your learning, since doing many tasks poorly is not advantageous. This will help you succeed in your next mathematics class!
      🔗
      🔗
    3. This system allows you to see the expectations of our course and how well you are meeting those expectations. If you would like to do better in this course, this system will make it clear exactly how you can do better (e.g., study a particular LT on which you have a low score and reassess on it!).
      🔗
      🔗
    If at any time you are unsure about how you are progressing in this course, I would be more than happy to speak with you about your progress!

    Subsection Course Policies

    Extra Credit.

    There will be no extra credit available in this course. If you are interested in raising your grade, take the time you would have spent on an extra credit assignment and spend it making sure you get more "regular credit" instead; for example, by studying for and scheduling a reassessment!
    🔗
    🔗

    Attendance and Class Recording.

    You are expected to attend each class meeting. If you must miss class, you are responsible for all announcements and material covered. Many of the topics we will cover build upon previously covered topics, so getting behind in this course can be problematic. With that said: If you are sick, please do not comes to class!. I will make sure you do not miss anything! To this end, an attempt will be made to record all class meetings. These recordings will be accessible only to the instructor and students in this course for the purpose of reviewing class discussions and presentations at a later time. These recordings could include your image, voice, and other forms of participation in this course. By enrolling in this course you are consenting to being recorded in this way.
    🔗
    🔗

    Class Cancelation.

    If class is canceled for any reason (including WSU closing), you will likely be expected to review some course material on your own before the next class meeting. All expectations will be communicated through email shortly after the cancellation is announced.

    🔗

    Email.

    I try to respond to emails within 24 hours; typically, emails received by 4:00pm will receive a response the same day, and those received after 4:00pm will receive a response the following day. You are encouraged to email me regarding any issues you are having in this course at any time; however, emails sent within 24 hours of an assessment may not get a response beforehand. So please plan accordingly!

    Studying.

    To succeed in this course, you will need to dedicate a significant amount of time to the course. Mathematics is learned by doing, meaning it is important to work numerous problems to fully understand the concepts we cover. The assigned work is the minimum amount you should be completing; if you feel like you don’t fully understand a concept, ask me for some additional problems. A general rule (for all college courses) is for each hour of class per week you spend a minimum of two hours outside of class on homework, studying, etc. Since this course meets three hours per week, you should plan to spend at least six hours outside of class per week working on course material.
    🔗

    If you anticipate issues related to the format or requirements of this course, please contact me as early as possible to discuss ways to ensure your full participation. If you believe formal, disability-related accommodations are appropriate, it is very important you register with the Student Accessibility Services office and notify me of your eligibility for such accommodations. We can then plan how to best coordinate your accommodations.

    Academic Integrity and AI Usage.

    The WSU Mathematics Department does not tolerate academic dishonesty! For an explanation of what is considered “academic dishonesty” at WSU, please see the policy included in the 2025-2026 Undergraduate Catalog. All instructors in the WSU Mathematics Department are required to report all incidents of academic dishonesty observed, detected, or otherwise determined to have occurred to the WSU Academic Central File. WSU requires some penalty be imposed for every reported violation of the academic honesty policy. All incidents of academic dishonesty in this course will result in (at least) a score of 0 on that assignment; additional penalties will be imposed at the instructor’s discretion and/or as required by the Mathematics Department Academic Honesty Policy. In this course, instances of academic dishonesty will include (but not limited to!):
    🔗
    • Submitting any assignment (including class prep assignments or homework) you did not complete yourself, either work produced by a classmate/friend or from an online third party (such as Chegg, Course Hero, etc.). Working with your peers is completely fine and strongly encouraged; however, any submissions should be a result of your own understanding of the subject matter based on this collaboration.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Using course materials, the internet, or a calculator in any unauthorized way during a quiz or the final exam.
      🔗
      🔗
    • AI cannot be used on graded assignments in this course unless otherwise specified.
      🔗
      🔗

    Subsection Learning Targets (LTs)

    Here is a list of the 22 LTs for this course. Most will only be referred to by number (as in, "This week’s quiz will cover LTs #12, 13 and 14.") so you will need to refer to this list frequently.
    🔗
    1. State the definitions of rational and irrational numbers; and use them along with other tools to prove that a given number is rational or irrational.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Prove properties of rational and irrational numbers, or provide counterexamples to show a property does not hold.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Perform various operations and solve problems involving rational and real numbers and their decimal expansions.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Classify a rational number as having a decimal expansion which is finite, simple-periodic, or delayed-periodic; and use properties of the three types to solve problems.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Perform basic operations on complex numbers, including addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, conjugation, finding the modulus and real/imaginary parts; and use them to prove algebraic properties of complex numbers.
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Find and use the polar form of a complex number and the distance formula to prove properties of complex numbers, including using equations to represent objects in the complex plane.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. State Euler’s formula and DeMoivre’s formula; and use them to solve problems and write proofs.
      🔗
      🔗
    8. State the definition of an isometry; and use it to prove results about isometries and congruence.
      🔗
      🔗
    9. Calculate the images of objects under translations; and use definitions and established properties to prove results about translations.
      🔗
      🔗
    10. Calculate the images of objects under rotations; and use definitions and established properties to prove results about rotations.
      🔗
      🔗
    11. State the Two Reflection Theorem; and use it to solve problems, including writing a composition of two reflections as a single isometry and writing an isometry as a composition of two reflections.
      🔗
      🔗
    12. Calculate the images of objects under reflections and glide reflections; and use definitions and established properties to prove results about reflections and glide reflections.
      🔗
      🔗
    13. Identify the type of a given isometry using properties such as fixed points, orientation-preservation (or lack thereof), and the parity of the number of reflections in a composition.
      🔗
      🔗
    14. Find equations of congruent graphs formed using various isometries; and solve problems involving them.
      🔗
      🔗
    15. State, prove, and use results from traditional congruence, such as the side-side-side criterion.
      🔗
      🔗
    16. Use the taxi-cab metric to calculate distance and prove properties, including connections with the standard Euclidean distance and isometries.
      🔗
      🔗
    17. State the definition of a similarity transformation; and use it to prove results about similarity transformations and similarity.
      🔗
      🔗
    18. Find equations of similar graphs formed using various similarity transformations.
      🔗
      🔗
    19. Prove facts about similar polygons; and solve problems involving them.
      🔗
      🔗
    20. Prove properties and solve problems relating to angle measure and basic right triangle trigonometry.
      🔗
      🔗
    21. Prove trigonometric identities, including the Law of Sines and Law of Cosines; and use them to solve problems involving triangles.
      🔗
      🔗
    22. State the definition of area; and use it to find the area of various polygonal regions, including justifying classic formulas for the areas of rectangles, triangles, parallelograms, trapezoids, and circles.
      🔗
      🔗
