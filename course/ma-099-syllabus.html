    Course Content.

    This course is a review of elementary algebra. The goal is to prepare you for your next mathematics course. Topics include advanced arithmetic, simplifying expressions, linear equations, exponents, polynomial algebra, factoring, and rational expressions and equations.     Subsection Assignments and Grading

    Standards Based Grading.

    We will be using standards based grading, which is an alternative approach to grading emphasizing progress and growth rather than an arbitrary weighted average of all assessments. Instead of using a percentage-based system, your grade will be determined by how well you display mastery of specific sorts of mathematical tasks called Learning Targets (LTs). This type of grading system allows students to make up for past poor performances by allowing for a limited number of reassessments without penalty, provides real-time feedback on their current understanding of course objectives, and directs and emphasizes long-term progress in key course objectives. All aspects of this course and its grading scheme will focus around the 22 LTs presented at the end of this syllabus. In short, your grade will be determined by your performance in four categories: class prep assignments, online homework, weekly in-class quizzes, and a cumulative final exam; detailed information about each of these categories, as well as how your course grade will be determined using these categories, can be found in the following sections of this syllabus. You will likely find this system to be unfamiliar, so I encourage you to ask questions about this approach to grading as soon as they arise! Please see the “Advantages of Standards Based Grading” section of this syllabus for more information about why we are using this system!
    🔗
    🔗

    Class Prep Assignments.

    A class prep assignment will be due before most class meetings consisting of a video introducing the topic for that class, followed by some basic questions asking you to demonstrate your understanding of the video. The class prep assignments will be due by 11:59pm on Mondays and Wednesdays (i.e., the night before each class meeting). No late attempts will be accepted for any reason and no reassessment is allowed. Class prep assignments will be graded on correctness, completeness, and effort (as determined by the instructor) and will be weighted equally. No scores will be dropped.
    🔗
    🔗

    Online Homework.

    There will be an online homework set for each LT opening on MOM after the class meeting when it is covered. Online homework sets are due by 11:59pm on Mondays. While the number of problems in each set will vary, your score will be considered as a percent and each set will be weighted equally when computing your online homework grade. No scores will be dropped. The online homework is set up so you can try most problems two times, then the correct answer will be shown. If you miss a problem and want to improve your score, you can click the "Try a similar problem" link, which will give you a new question of the same type. You can keep on working on versions of a question until you get it correct. You must earn at least 50% on an assignment for your score to count towards your grade; any set in which you earn less than 50% will be counted as a 0. Assignments will remain available in "Practice" mode after their due date to provide additional practice of concepts; new versions of problems can be generated and answered, but scores will not be saved.
    🔗
    To account for any unexpected emergencies or technical issues that may occur, we will utilize the LatePass feature in MOM. A LatePass can be redeemed for an automatic 48-hour extension on a homework. To use a LatePass, click the "Use LatePass" button next to the appropriate assignment on MOM and the platform will automatically extend the due date for that assignment to 48 hours after the original due date. Each student will be given 14 LatePasses to use as they wish throughout the semester. Note a maximum of two LatePasses can be used on any single assignment. LatePasses can NOT be used on any online homework set which you view in "Practice" mode prior to redeeming the LatePass. If circumstances require a more substantial extension, contact your instructor as soon as possible to discuss this possibility.
    🔗
    Although you will be entering only answers, I encourage you to work out each problem carefully on paper. This will help you follow your reasoning when you are reviewing for quizzes. I strongly encourage you to start each assignment right away so you will have ample time to ask questions. The exercises are intended to both provide practice of concepts covered in class as well as extend these concepts beyond what is done in class; in particular, you should expect to find some of the exercises difficult. Many quiz problems will be based on the homework exercises, including the more difficult ones, so you should aim to understand the underlying idea for each assigned exercise. You should expect to spend several hours each week working on homework.
    🔗
    If you are having trouble on a problem, you may use the "Message Instructor" link, which will allow you to send me a message through MOM; the specific problem you are working on will be automatically included in the message. I can then reply to the message with some guidance. These messages are internal to MOM and not email, so my reply will go to your messages when you login to MOM.

    Quizzes.

    We will have a quiz at the end of every Thursday class (and possibly some Tuesdays toward the end of the semester) where you will have an opportunity to demonstrate your understanding of the course LTs. Each quiz question will address a single LT; you will receive a score for each individual question based on how well you showed your understanding of the corresponding LT according to the following rubric:
    🔗
    Score Description
    4 Mastery of the LT exceeding expections. Provides clear understanding with complete and clear communication. Any error is trivial.
    3 Adequate understanding of the LT meeting expectations. Provides an adequate understanding with correct work and clear explanations. Some revisions or expansion is needed, but no significant gaps or errors are present.
    2 Partial understanding of LT falling below expectations. Provides a partial understanding, but significant gaps remain. Needs further work, more review, and/or improved explanation.
    1 Little understanding of LT falling significantly below expectations. Provides little to no understanding with either fragmentary work, significant errors or omissions, or too many errors.
    0 No understanding of the LT or LT was not attempted.
    Each LT will be assessed twice on in-class quizzes before the end of the semester, typically during consecutive Thursday classes. A limited number of reassessments will be allowed; see next paragraph for details. Your grade for each LT will be the average of your highest two attempts among the two in-class quizzes and any reassessment attempts you complete.
    🔗
    In order to continually emphasize progress and growth, you may try to improve your score on any LT by reassessing on it. In other words, if you did not perform well on a LT on an in-class quiz, you may reattempt a quiz question on that LT again. Reassessments can be taken in-class during a future quiz or in my office during office hours or other times set aside for reassessments. Reassessments must be scheduled at least 24 hours in advance using the Google Form posted on MOM to ensure I will have the appropriate reassessment ready! ou must submit this form 24 hours prior in order to be allowed to reassess; simply showing up at my office will not suffice! You may reassess no more than two LTs per week. In addition to these weekly reassessment opportunities, two of our Thursday class meetings, March 12 and April 30, will be dedicated “reassessment days.” No new material will be covered on these days, instead the class time will be dedicated to allowing students to reassess on up to 10 LTs each in addition to the usual LT quiz for that class meeting.
    🔗
    Let’s look at an example of how the LT Quizzes and reassessments will work! Suppose Alex earns a 0 on a specific LT the first time it is assessed and a 3 the second time it is assessed; at this point Alex’s score for this LT is a 1.5 (the average of their two attempts, 0 and 3). Alex then requests to reassess on this LT in an office hour the following week. Alex earns a 4 on this reassessment; now their score for this LT is a 3.5 (the average of the highest two attempts, 3 and 4; the initial 0 is no longer counted!). In this example, Alex has learned from their mistake, and shown growth and progress towards mastery of this LT. Their score reflects this growth!

    Advantages of Standards Based Grading.

    This grading system may be unfamiliar to you, and therefore cause some level of confusion or stress. Please try to keep an open mind about this grading system and remember things are not bad simply because they are unfamiliar! From my point of view as a mathematics professor who has taught a vast number of courses for over a decade, including this one multiple times, there are three major advantages of this standards based grading system over a traditional weighted-average system:
    🔗
    1. You will have several opportunities to display your understanding of the material. You will be assessed on each of the LTs twice on quizzes and have the ability to reassess on them, so you do not need to worry if you do not completely understand a LT the first time you encounter it! If it takes you a bit longer to grasp a concept, you will not be penalized as long as you make the effort to reassess!
      🔗
      🔗
    2. This approach stresses working out problems in a completely correct way to emphasize your learning, since doing many tasks poorly is not advantageous. This will help you succeed in your next mathematics class!
      🔗
      🔗
    3. This system allows you to see the expectations of our course and how well you are meeting those expectations. If you would like to do better in this course, this system will make it clear exactly how you can do better (e.g., study a particular LT on which you have a low score and reassess on it!).
      🔗
      🔗
    If at any time you are unsure about how you are progressing in this course, I would be more than happy to speak with you about your progress!

    Studying.

    To succeed in this course, you will need to dedicate a significant amount of time to the course. Mathematics is learned by doing, meaning it is important to work numerous problems to fully understand the concepts we cover. The assigned work is the minimum amount you should be completing; if you feel like you don’t fully understand a concept, ask me for some additional problems. A general rule (for all college courses) is for each hour of class per week you spend a minimum of two hours outside of class on homework, studying, etc. Since this course meets three hours per week, you should plan to spend at least six hours outside of class per week working on math.

    Academic Integrity and AI Usage.

    The WSU Mathematics Department does not tolerate academic dishonesty! For an explanation of what is considered “academic dishonesty” at WSU, please see the policy included in the 2025-2026 Undergraduate Catalog. All instructors in the WSU Mathematics Department are required to report all incidents of academic dishonesty observed, detected, or otherwise determined to have occurred to the WSU Academic Central File. WSU requires some penalty be imposed for every reported violation of the academic honesty policy. All incidents of academic dishonesty in this course will result in (at least) a score of 0 on that assignment; additional penalties will be imposed at the instructor’s discretion and/or as required by the Mathematics Department Academic Honesty Policy. In this course, instances of academic dishonesty will include (but not limited to!):

Working with your peers is completely fine and strongly encouraged; however, any submissions should be a result of your own understanding of the subject matter based on this collaboration.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Using course materials, the internet, or a calculator in any unauthorized way during a weekly quiz or the final exam (including looking up answers online, viewing notes/texts/examples on MOM or another site, communicating with other students).
      🔗
      🔗
    • AI cannot be used on graded assignments in this course unless otherwise specified.

    Students will be able to solve problems involving
    🔗
    1. Operations with integers and rational numbers: topics include computation with integers and negative rationals, the use of absolute values, and ordering.

      🔗
    2. Operations with algebraic expressions: topics include the evaluation of simple formulas and expressions, adding and subtracting monomials and polynomials, multiplying and dividing monomials and polynomials, the evaluation of positive rational roots and exponents, simplifying algebraic fractions, and factoring.

      🔗
    3.     Subsection Learning Targets (LTs)

    Here is a list of the 22 LTs for this course. Most will only be referred to by number (as in, "This week’s quiz will cover LTs #12, 13 and 14.") so you will need to refer to this list frequently.
    🔗
    1. Add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Evaluate and simplify expressions using the order of operations.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Evaluate numbers and simplify expressions involving exponents, absolute values, and square roots.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Locate numbers on the number line and answer questions about their relative positions.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Solve linear equations with whole number coefficients.
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Solve linear equations involving fractions.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Evaluate formulas at given values and solve them for a given variable.
      🔗
      🔗
    8. Solve word problems by setting up and solving an equation.
      🔗
      🔗
    9. Simplify expressions using various properties of exponents.
      🔗
      🔗
    10. Add and subtract polynomials.
      🔗
      🔗
    11. Multiply polynomials, including squaring a binomial.
      🔗
      🔗
    12. Divide polynomials by a monomial.
      🔗
      🔗
    13. Identify the greatest common factor (GCF) of a polynomial, and factor it from a polynomial.
      🔗
      🔗
    14. Use factoring by grouping to completely factor a polynomial.
      🔗
      🔗
    15. Factor trinomials of the form \(x^2+bx+c\text{,}\) including ones with GCFs.

      🔗
    16. Factor a difference of squares, including ones with GCFs.
      🔗
      🔗
    17. Solve quadratic equations using the zero-product property and factoring.
      🔗
      🔗
    18. Simplify a rational expression completely.
      🔗
      🔗
    19. Multiply and divide rational expressions, and simplify completely.
      🔗
      🔗
    20. Add and subtract rational expressions with like denominators, and simplify completely.
      🔗
      🔗
    21. Find the lowest common denominator (LCD) for rational expressions, and use them to add and subtract rational expressions with unlike denominators.
      🔗
      🔗
    22. Solve rational equations.
