    Course Content.

    The primary goal of this course is to deepen your understanding of the geometry and measurement (and, a bit of the probability and statistics) strand of K--8 mathematics. This will often involve studying mathematics beyond the K--8 level to help future teachers gain a better perspective of how the topics and concepts they teach fit into the broader mathematical structure. Strengthening your understanding will help you to be more effective as a teacher. The course will provide a careful, mathematically accurate development of geometry and measurement (and a bit of probability and statistics) and help you teach the subject in a coherent and meaningful way. Mathematics has a structure that is logical -- everything has a reason. Understanding math well is the key to being able to teach it well. Course topics include measurement, geometric figures, unknown angles, deductive geometry, area, volume/surface area, similarity, introductory probability, and data analysis.

    Subsection Assignments and Grading

    Standards Based Grading.

    In this course we will be using standards based grading, which is an alternative approach to grading that emphasizes progress and growth rather than an arbitrary weighted average of all assessments. Instead of using a percentage-based system, your grade will be determined by how well you display mastery of specific sorts of mathematical tasks called Learning Targets (LTs). This type of grading system allows students to make up for past poor performances by allowing for a limited number of reassessments without penalty, provides real-time feedback on their current understanding of course objectives, and directs and emphasizes long-term progress in key course objectives. All aspects of this course and its grading scheme will focus around the 22 LTs presented at the end of this syllabus. In short, your grade will be determined by your performance in three categories: preparation and participation, in-class quizzes, and a cumulative final exam; detailed information about each of these categories, as well as how your course grade will be determined using these categories, can be found in the following sections of this syllabus. You will likely find this system to be unfamiliar, so I encourage you to ask questions about this approach to grading as soon as they arise! Please see the ``Advantages of Standards Based Grading’’ section of this syllabus for more information about why we are using this system in this course!
    🔗
    🔗

    Preparation and Participation.

    This is the first category contributing towards your course grade. There will be two primary types of assessments considered in this category:
    🔗
    Reading Guides
    🔗
    A reading guide will be assigned to be completed before most class meetings. Each will consist of a reading assignment from the required text and questions designed to help you take good notes. Completing a reading guide before class means you are prepared with all relevant definitions and basic examples worked out in front of you, and allows us to spend class time discussing more difficult aspects of the material and working together on problems! Our work in class will assume you have completed the reading guide; failure to do so could contribute to confusion about the course material. You will complete each reading guide in your own notes, then scan your pages into a single PDF and submit through MOM. Reading guides will be due by 11:59pm on Mondays and Wednesdays (i.e., the night before each class meeting). No late attempts will be accepted for any reason and no reassessment is allowed. Reading guides will be graded on completeness and effort as determined by the instructor.
    🔗
    Class Participation
    🔗
    We will meet weekly on Tuesdays and Thursdays, 8:30--9:45am. Attendance and active participation, including working collaboratively with classmates, during these meetings are expected. The class meetings will consist of a mix of lecture and active learning activities based on the course material from the reading guide. You will earn a weekly participation grade; to earn credit for this, you must fully participate throughout the entire class meeting -- this means being in class ready to go at 8:30am, having phones/laptops/other devices put away, and only your notes and other material related to this course and the topic at hand on your desk.
    🔗
    Your overall Preparation and Participation score will be determined by your reading guide scores and class participation scores averaged together.
    🔗
    🔗

    Quizzes.

    This category is based on your demonstration of this course’s 22 learning targets as assessed on in-class quizzes given at the end of every Thursday class. Each quiz question will address a single LT; you will receive a score for each individual question based on how well you showed your understanding of the corresponding LT according to the following rubric:
    🔗
    Score Description
    4 Mastery of the LT exceeding expections. Provides clear understanding with complete and clear communication. Any error is trivial.
    3 Adequate understanding of the LT meeting expectations. Provides an adequate understanding with correct work and clear explanations. Some revisions or expansion is needed, but no significant gaps or errors are present.
    2 Partial understanding of LT falling below expectations. Provides a partial understanding, but significant gaps remain. Needs further work, more review, and/or improved explanation.
    1 Little understanding of LT falling significantly below expectations. Provides little to no understanding with either fragmentary work, significant errors or omissions, or too many errors.
    0 No understanding of the LT or LT was not attempted.
    Each LT will be assessed twice on in-class quizzes before the end of the semester. A limited number of reassessments will be allowed (see next paragraph for details). Your grade for each LT will be the average of your highest two attempts among the two in-class quizzes and any reassessment attempts you complete.
    🔗
    In order to continually emphasize progress and growth, you may try to improve your score on any LT by reassessing on it. In other words, if you did not perform well on a LT on an in-class quiz, you may reattempt a question on that LT again. Reassessments can be taken in-class during a future quiz or in my office during office hours or other times set aside for reassessments. \textbf{Reassessments must be scheduled at least 24 hours in advance using the Google Form posted on MOM} to ensure I will have the appropriate reassessment ready! You \textbf{must} submit this form 24 hours prior in order to be allowed to reassess; simply showing up at my office will not suffice! You may reassess no more than two LTs per week. In addition to these weekly reassessment opportunities, two of our Thursday class meetings, March 12 and April 30, will be dedicated ``reassessment days.’’ No new material will be covered on these days, instead the class time will be dedicated to allowing students to reassess on up to 10 LTs each in addition to the usual LT quiz for that class meeting.
    🔗
    Let’s look at an example of how the LT Quizzes and reassessments will work! Suppose Alex earns a 0 on a specific LT the first time it is assessed and a 3 the second time it is assessed; at this point Alex’s score for this LT is a 1.5 (the average of their two attempts, 0 and 3). Alex then requests to reassess on this LT in an office hour the following week. Alex drops by to reassess on the LT and earns a 4; now their score for this LT is a 3.5 (the average of the highest two attempts, 3 and 4) -- meaning the initial 0 is no longer counted! In this example, Alex has learned from their mistake, and shown growth and progress towards mastery of this LT. Their score reflects this growth!
    🔗
    A collection of practice problems will be available for each of the LTs to provide a sample of the types of things you might be asked on the LT Quizzes, as well as provide some zero-stakes practice of the various concepts we’ve discussed. These practice problems will not be collected, nor will they contribute towards your course grade in any direct way; however, in many cases, practice will be necessary to master the LTs in this course! I will happily provide feedback on your work on any of the practice problems, if desired; simply email me your work.
    🔗
    🔗

There will be no reassessments for the final exam! A make-up final exam will be given at the instructor’s discretion, and only when there is a legitimate reason for missing the original final exam. Working or arriving late for class are not legitimate reasons for missing the final!

    Advantages of Standards Based Grading.

    This grading system may be unfamiliar to you, and therefore cause some level of confusion or stress. Please try to keep an open mind about this grading system and remember things are not bad simply because they are unfamiliar! From my point of view as a mathematics professor who has taught a vast number of courses for over a decade, including this one multiple times, there are three major advantages of this standards based grading system over a traditional weighted-average system:
    🔗
    1. You will have several opportunities to display your understanding of the material. You will be assessed on each of the LTs twice on quizzes and have the ability to reassess on them, so you do not need to worry if you do not completely understand a LT the first time you encounter it! If it takes you a bit longer to grasp a concept, you will not be penalized as long as you make the effort to reassess!
      🔗
      🔗
    2. This approach stresses working out problems in a completely correct way to emphasize your learning, since doing many tasks poorly is not advantageous. This will help you succeed in your next mathematics class!
      🔗
      🔗
    3. This system allows you to see the expectations of our course and how well you are meeting those expectations. If you would like to do better in this course, this system will make it clear exactly how you can do better (e.g., study a particular LT on which you have a low score and reassess on it!).
      🔗
      🔗
    If at any time you are unsure about how you are progressing in this course, I would be more than happy to speak with you about your progress!

      🔗
      🔗
    15. Determine whether two triangles are similar, and use similar triangles to solve problems.
      🔗
      🔗
    16. Convert between units of area and explain how scaling affects area.
      🔗
      🔗
    17. Solve problems involving pi and the circumference of circles.
      🔗
      🔗
    18. Solve problems involving the area of a circle.
      🔗
      🔗
    19. Answer questions and solve problems about units of volume, and calculate basic volumes.
      🔗
      🔗
    20. Solve problems using the volumes of cylinders, cones, pyramids, and spheres.
      🔗
      🔗
    21. Calculate the mean, median, and mode of a data set, and solve related problems.

    22. Develop a probability model for an experiment and its sample space, and use it to determine probabilities of events.
